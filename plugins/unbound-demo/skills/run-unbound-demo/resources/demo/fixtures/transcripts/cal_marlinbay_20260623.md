# Marlin Bay Cruises — discovery before the proposal

**Date:** 2026-06-23 · **Attendees:** Diane Halloran (Marlin Bay, VP revenue operations), Priscilla Nunez (Marlin Bay, sales enablement lead), Nadia Ellis (UnCompany)

---

**Nadia:** Thanks both. Before I write a proposal I want it built on your numbers, not ours. So start me at the beginning. What are you running on the group-charter desk today?

**Diane:** Salesforce for the opportunity. Outlook for mail and calendar. Charter quotes and the deck templates live in SharePoint. Calls are on Zoom, recorded maybe half the time.

**Priscilla:** And ChatGPT Enterprise. IT rolled the seats out in January. Most of the desk has opened it twice.

**Nadia:** What is held together with a spreadsheet?

**Diane:** The forecast. I rebuild it Thursday night out of whatever the sellers backfilled that week.

**Nadia:** Walk me through a group booking. The enquiry lands — then what?

**Priscilla:** It hits the group desk inbox. A seller picks it up, quotes off the charter sheet, books a site visit if it is big enough. Contract goes through legal.

**Nadia:** Where does it stall?

**Diane:** Two places. Turnaround on the first reply — we are at two days and it should be same-day. And after the site visit, when a seller owes four people something and gets to two of them. A deal that stalls on a Tuesday falls out of view until somebody chases it.

**Nadia:** And what is costing the desk the most time?

**Diane:** Admin. We clocked it. About ninety minutes per seller per day on group-charter. Nobody believes that number until they see the timestamps.

**Priscilla:** Every seller also writes their follow-up from scratch. The same charter question gets three different answers depending on who took the call. That is my problem rather than Diane's, but it is the same root.

**Nadia:** It is the same root. The agent reads the call and the thread and drafts the follow-up inside the seat they already have, so the answer comes out of the conversation instead of out of whoever is writing that day. Priscilla, that stops being a training problem.

**Diane:** Then say the proposal maps to three numbers. Admin minutes per seller per day. Enquiry turnaround. Forecast accuracy. Those are what I am judged on.

**Nadia:** It maps to those three and nothing else. And it does not add a line to your stack — it runs in the ChatGPT seats you already pay for, direct, and one install fee from us. When the install closes the engagement closes, and you keep the config.

**Diane:** Good. Then the last thing, and it is the thing that decides it.

**Nadia:** What makes this a clear yes?

**Diane:** The exec review. Send me something I can put in front of them — a comparable operator who installed this and is still running it a couple of quarters later, with the numbers. Our own projections carry nothing at this stage.

**Nadia:** You will have it. Who else needs to see it happen?

**Diane:** Owen. He runs the floor and he has watched two rollouts die on it. He is not blocking the proposal. But eventually he wants a number off our own sellers, not a vendor slide.

**Nadia:** Reasonable. I will hold that read as a follow-up rather than gate the proposal on it, and we report back once the first week is measured. So: a recap confirming the three metrics, the durability case study attached for the exec review, and a working session the week after to walk the draft proposal against those KPIs. I will offer times for you and Priscilla.

**Diane:** That works. Keep it tight. I will be reading it between meetings.
