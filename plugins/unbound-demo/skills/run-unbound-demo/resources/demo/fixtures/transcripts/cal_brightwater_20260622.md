# Brightwater Media — Project Lighthouse discovery and install scope

**Date:** 2026-06-22 · **Attendees:** Lars Vandenberg (Brightwater, revenue operations lead), Femke Janssen (Brightwater, Salesforce owner), Nadia Ellis (UnCompany)

---

**Nadia:** Thanks for making time. Two things today. I want to understand how the desk actually runs before we scope anything. Then we agree what the install has to prove.

**Lars:** Fair enough. Where do you want to start?

**Nadia:** What are you running today? Not the org chart — the tools a seller touches on a Tuesday.

**Lars:** Salesforce, one org per masthead, three of them. Gong on the calls. Gmail and Google Calendar. The rate cards live in a Drive folder. And everyone got a Claude seat in the corporate rollout in March.

**Nadia:** How much of that is genuinely connected?

**Femke:** Salesforce and Gong talk to each other. Nothing else does. The pipeline review runs off a spreadsheet I rebuild every Monday morning, by hand, out of three orgs.

**Nadia:** Then that spreadsheet is most of the answer. Walk me through a deal. A rate-card conversation comes in — where does it go?

**Lars:** Seller takes the call. Writes the follow-up when they get to it, which is that evening or the next morning. Logs the opportunity in Salesforce on Friday. If they log it.

**Nadia:** And where does it get stuck, or fall out of view?

**Lars:** Between the call and the log. A deal moves on Tuesday and nobody sees it until Femke's spreadsheet on Monday. By then the seller has forgotten half of what was agreed.

**Femke:** It is also why the three stage fields we actually use are wrong more often than they are right.

**Nadia:** Understood. Next one, and it is the one that decides the number. What is costing you the most time?

**Lars:** Post-call admin, easily. Every seller says it. Though I will be honest — I half-believe it is not costing us as much as they claim. They complain about everything. You would have to show me it is.

**Nadia:** That is the right challenge, and I would rather prove it than assert it. The shape is this. Run a baseline week before anything goes live and count the admin minutes each seller spends after a call today. Then count approval rate and minutes reclaimed across the first live week, and put the two side by side. There is a worksheet that defines what gets counted, so nobody moves the goalposts afterwards.

**Lars:** The baseline is doable. It is an internal ask — thirty-one sellers logging for a week, and Femke's team pulling the Salesforce edit history. Let me take it to the desk leads.

**Nadia:** Take it. It runs in parallel. The install does not wait on it.

**Femke:** So what are we installing, exactly? I need to know what touches Salesforce.

**Nadia:** The agent reads the call and the thread, drafts the next move on each open deal, and the seller approves it in one pass. It runs inside the Claude seat they already open — no new interface, no new login, nothing for the floor to relearn. On the write side it routes by masthead, so it needs your three org IDs and the field mapping.

**Femke:** I will send the org IDs, the mapping for the three stage fields, and two example opportunity records over Slack after this.

**Lars:** And what happens to the seats? We are already paying for Claude.

**Nadia:** You keep paying for those direct, at source. We charge one install fee and that is the whole commercial relationship. When the install closes the engagement closes, and the config is a set of files you own.

**Nadia:** Last question. What would make this a clear yes for you?

**Lars:** Minutes back, in a number I can put in front of our COO without caveats. And Femke's Monday morning going away.

**Femke:** That would be the day.

**Lars:** The desk leads have to see it work on real deals first, though. They run the floor. If they say no it does not matter what I think.

**Nadia:** Then that is what we build toward. One more working session before the in-person deep-dive, with the desk leads in the room, walking the first drafts against real open deals. Can we find time in the next couple of weeks?

**Femke:** Works for me. Send some times.

**Lars:** Same. And put that worksheet in the recap. I want to read what it measures before I ask thirty-one people to log a baseline week.

**Nadia:** Will do. I will send the follow-up to both of you — the scope, what each of us is waiting on, the baseline-week option, and candidate times for the next session.
