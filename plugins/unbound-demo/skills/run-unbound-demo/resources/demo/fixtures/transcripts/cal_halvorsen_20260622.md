# Halvorsen & Pike — discovery and evaluation scope

**Date:** 2026-06-22 · **Attendees:** Eleanor Voss (Halvorsen & Pike, VP revenue operations), Rohan Mehta (Halvorsen & Pike, principal systems engineer), Nadia Ellis (UnCompany)

---

**Nadia:** Thanks for the time. I want to understand how the advisory desk runs before we agree what the evaluation measures. Start me on the stack.

**Eleanor:** Salesforce Financial Services Cloud. Outlook for mail and calendar. Client calls are on Teams and the transcripts land in OneDrive. Proposals and mandate documents sit in SharePoint.

**Rohan:** And Copilot. The firm standardized on it last year. Every advisor has a seat and it went through a full review to get there.

**Nadia:** Anything held together with a spreadsheet?

**Eleanor:** The commitments log. What each advisor promised a client, and by when. I keep it because Salesforce will not hold it in a form anybody reads.

**Nadia:** Walk me through a mandate. A referral comes in — how does it move?

**Eleanor:** Intro call. Discovery meeting with the analyst. Proposal. Compliance review. Signature.

**Nadia:** Where does it get stuck, or fall out of view?

**Rohan:** Compliance, usually. The quieter one is the handoff. The advisor takes the call, the analyst builds the model, and three weeks later nobody can say what was actually promised on that call.

**Eleanor:** Which is what my spreadsheet is for, and it is a bad instrument.

**Nadia:** What is costing you the most time each week?

**Eleanor:** The hour after every client call. The advisor reconstructs what was said, writes the follow-up, updates the record. They do it at six in the evening, so they do it badly, and the record ends up thinner than the call was.

**Nadia:** That hour is the one we are after. The agent reads the transcript and the thread, drafts the follow-up and the record update, and the advisor approves in one pass. It runs inside the Copilot seat they already have. No new interface, no second login, nothing new in front of the advisor.

**Rohan:** Let me be the skeptic, because I am. My worry is not the drafting. It is that something reads a client thread and then sends. Show me where the approval sits, and what it can and cannot touch. Compliance will ask me before they ask you.

**Nadia:** The answer is narrow. It drafts. It never sends. Every action waits on one human approval, in one pass. It brings no logins and opens no connections of its own — it reads through the access that Copilot seat already has. Nothing lands in your systems uninvited.

**Rohan:** And I am not putting a second AI vendor through that review.

**Nadia:** You are not adding one. You keep paying Microsoft for the seats, direct. One install fee from us, once.

**Rohan:** Who holds the keys when the engagement ends?

**Nadia:** You do. The config is a set of files you keep. The engagement closes when the install closes and there is no rent on the workflow after that. I sent the security and ownership overview across ahead of Dana's review, so her team reads it early rather than as a late gate.

**Eleanor:** Good. Dana will want it in writing. So what do you need from us to run the evaluation?

**Nadia:** Two things. A baseline week, so the evaluation turns on your own desk rather than on a slide. And a threshold from Rohan, up front — the number he will accept as proof. Beyond that, what would make this a clear yes, and who else has to see it happen?

**Eleanor:** Dana signs off on governance. And the four advisors on the private-client desk have to want it. If they do not open it, none of this matters.

**Nadia:** Then they are in the room. Can we hold a working session the week of the 22nd to lock the scope and that threshold? I will send times and the measurement worksheet, so Rohan can read the methodology first.

**Rohan:** Send it. If the approval path holds up under Dana's review, I am open to it.

**Nadia:** Will do. I will follow up to you and Eleanor with the baseline offer, the worksheet, and candidate times for the scoping session.
